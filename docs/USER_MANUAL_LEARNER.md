# Professional English Growth Hub - Learner User Manual

## 1. What is Professional English Growth Hub?

Professional English Growth Hub is a personal English learning companion designed for adult professionals. It helps you organize your daily practice, track your progress, identify your mistakes, and work with your teacher to improve your professional English skills.

This app is a **tracker and organizer**, not a full English teacher. It helps you stay consistent, focused, and accountable in your learning journey.

---

## 2. Who is this app for?

This app is designed for:
- Adult professionals
- Current level: B1+ to B2
- Target level: Strong B2 to Early C1
- Focus: Business English, meetings, speaking confidence, writing, listening, pronunciation, grammar accuracy, and professional communication

If your goals include improving your English for work, meetings, presentations, and professional communication, this app is for you.

---

## 3. What this app can help you do

- **Plan your learning**: Daily, weekly, and monthly plans with clear tasks
- **Track your progress**: See your improvement over time in all skill areas
- **Practice speaking**: Use structured topics to improve your speaking fluency
- **Identify mistakes**: Track your common errors and review them regularly
- **Get recommendations**: Receive personalized activity suggestions based on your progress
- **Work with a teacher**: Share your data with your teacher for focused coaching
- **Monitor your level**: See your official level and suggested level based on your performance

---

## 4. What this app cannot do

This app is **not**:
- A certified CEFR assessment platform
- A full AI English teacher
- A replacement for human interaction
- A source of graded reading or listening materials
- A provider of automatic feedback on your speaking or writing
- A complete English course with lessons and exercises

You will need to use this app alongside other learning resources for the best results.

## 5. First-time Setup

### 5.1 Set Your Learner Profile

1. Go to **Profile Settings**
2. Enter your name
3. Set your current level (be honest - this helps with accurate recommendations)
4. Set your target level
5. Select your main goal
6. Choose your primary focus areas

### 5.2 Choose Plan Intensity

Select a plan that fits your schedule:
- **Light**: 30-45 minutes/day, 4 days/week, 12 months
- **Standard**: 60-90 minutes/day, 5-6 days/week, 9 months
- **Intensive**: 90-120 minutes/day, 6 days/week, 6 months
- **Sprint**: 120 minutes/day, 6-7 days/week, 3 months (quick improvement for work communication)

### 5.3 Check Local Storage / Backup Note

**Important:** This app stores all your data in your browser's Local Storage.

- Your data stays on your device
- Clearing browser data will delete everything
- **Always export a backup before clearing cache or switching browsers**
- Export your data regularly (weekly or monthly)

## 9. How to Use Speaking Topics

### 9.1 Search and Filter

1. Go to **Speaking Topics**
2. Use the search bar to find specific topics
3. Filter by category, difficulty, or duration
4. Click "Random Topic" for variety

### 9.2 Speaking Structure

Each speaking topic includes a structure to follow:
1. Introduction
2. Background
3. Current Status
4. Challenge
5. Action Taken
6. Next Step
7. Conclusion

Use this structure to organize your speaking and make it professional.

### 9.3 Add Notes

After completing a speaking topic:
- Add notes on what went well
- Note phrases you want to reuse
- Write what you struggled with

### 9.4 Mark as Completed

- Check the checkbox when finished
- This updates your progress
- Completed topics are tracked in your history

### 9.5 Record Yourself Externally

The app does not record your voice. To practice effectively:
1. Use your phone's voice recorder
2. Record yourself speaking for 2-3 minutes
3. Listen back and identify mistakes
4. Add those mistakes to the Mistakes section

## 10. How to Use Mistakes

### 10.1 Add a Mistake

1. Go to **Mistakes**
2. Click "Add Mistake"
3. Enter the wrong sentence
4. Enter the correct sentence
5. Choose a category (Grammar, Vocabulary, Pronunciation, etc.)
6. Choose the source (Self, Teacher, Evaluation)
7. Set status: New, Practicing, or Fixed

### 10.2 Wrong vs Correct Sentence

Be specific about the error:
- **Wrong**: "I am agree with your point."
- **Correct**: "I agree with your point."

### 10.3 Categories

Use categories to organize your mistakes:
- Grammar
- Vocabulary
- Pronunciation
- Fluency
- Business Phrases
- Professional Communication

### 10.4 Sources

Track where mistakes happen:
- Self: Mistakes you noticed yourself
- Teacher: Mistakes identified by your teacher
- Evaluation: Mistakes found during evaluations

### 10.5 Status Management

- **New**: Recently added, not yet practiced
- **Practicing**: You are actively working on this mistake
- **Fixed**: You have mastered this and no longer make it

### 10.6 Review Mistakes Weekly

Once a week:
1. Go to **Mistakes**
2. Review all active mistakes
3. Practice sentences with those mistakes
4. Mark as "Fixed" when you no longer make the error

## 12. How to Use Listening Practice

### 12.1 Add a Listening Entry

1. Go to **Listening Practice**
2. Click "Add Listening Entry"
3. Describe the main idea
4. List useful phrases you heard
5. Write a summary
6. Note the accent and difficulty level

### 12.2 Main Idea

What was the listening about? Be concise.

### 12.3 Useful Phrases

Write down new expressions or phrases you want to remember.

### 12.4 Summary

Write a brief summary of what you understood.

### 12.5 Accent and Difficulty

## 13. How to Use Pronunciation Practice

### 13.1 Add a Sentence

1. Go to **Pronunciation Practice**
2. Click "Add Sentence"
3. Enter a sentence to practice
4. Choose a focus area (stress, intonation, specific sounds)
5. Set number of repetitions

### 13.2 Focus Areas

Choose what to work on:
- **Stress**: Word and sentence stress patterns
- **Intonation**: Rising and falling patterns
- **Specific Sounds**: Problematic sounds (th, r, v, etc.)

### 13.3 Repetitions

Practice the sentence multiple times to improve muscle memory.

### 13.4 Score Yourself

After practicing, rate your clarity and confidence (1-5).

## 14. How to Use Evaluation

### 14.1 Weekly or Monthly Evaluation

Complete an evaluation:
1. Go to **Evaluation**
2. Rate each skill (Speaking, Listening, Grammar, Vocabulary, Pronunciation, Writing, Fluency, Confidence)
3. Add notes on your progress
4. Identify your weakest skill to focus on

### 14.2 Skill Scores

Rate each skill from 0-100:
- **0-40**: Beginner
- **41-60**: Elementary
- **61-75**: Intermediate
- **76-90**: Upper Intermediate
- **91-100**: Advanced

### 14.3 Notes

Add reflections on your progress, challenges, and achievements.

### 14.4 Next Focus

Based on your scores, identify which skill needs the most work.

## 15. How to Use Level & Assessment

### 15.1 Official Level

This is your confirmed level. It only changes when:
- You manually update it
- You accept a suggested level

### 15.2 Suggested Level

The app calculates this based on your:
- Evaluation scores
- Mistakes patterns
- Progress trends

**Important:** This is **not** an official CEFR assessment. It is guidance only.

### 15.3 Why Suggested Level is Not Certification

- The app does not have CEFR-certified assessments
- There is no external validation
- The calculation is based on your self-assessment
- For official certification, take a recognized CEFR test

### 15.4 Accept Suggested Level Only When Confident

Only accept a suggested level when:
- You have consistent evidence across multiple evaluations
- Your teacher agrees with the assessment
- You have demonstrated this level in real situations

## 22. Common Mistakes When Using the App

### 22.1 Not Being Honest About Your Level

- Don't set your level higher than it actually is
- Be realistic so recommendations are accurate
- Your teacher will know your true level anyway

### 22.2 Skipping the Daily Plan

- The daily plan is designed for consistent progress
- Skipping days breaks your momentum
- Consistency is more important than intensity

### 22.3 Not Recording Yourself

- Self-recording is the best way to hear your own mistakes
- You cannot improve what you don't hear
- Record yourself at least 3 times per week

### 22.4 Not Reviewing Mistakes

- Mistakes only help if you practice them
- Review active mistakes weekly
- Mark mistakes as "Fixed" when you've mastered them

### 22.5 Not Doing Evaluations

- Evaluations help you track progress
- Do them weekly or bi-weekly
- They show you which skills need work

### 22.6 Not Backing Up Data

- Local Storage can be cleared accidentally
- Export your data regularly
- Don't risk losing your progress

### 22.7 Trying to Do Too Much

- Start with the Standard or Light intensity
- It's better to be consistent than overwhelmed
- You can increase intensity later

### 22.8 Not Using the Structure

- The speaking structure is there to help you
- Follow it for more professional communication
- It makes your speaking more organized

### 22.9 Not Working with a Teacher

- Self-directed learning has limits
- A teacher provides feedback you cannot give yourself
- Even monthly sessions make a big difference

### 22.10 Not Practicing Outside the App

- The app organizes your learning
- But you must practice in real situations too
- Use English in your actual work whenever possible

- Study at the same time each day

### 23.2 Record Yourself Regularly

- Record yourself speaking 3-4 times per week
- Listen back and identify mistakes
- Add those mistakes to your tracker

- Work with a teacher even once a month

## 24. Limitations

This app has important limitations you should understand:

1. **No Graded Content:** You must find your own reading and listening materials

2. **No Automatic Feedback:** You must get feedback from a teacher or use other tools

3. **No CEFR Certification:** This is not an official assessment platform

4. **No Human Interaction:** All practice is simulated, not real communication

5. **No Spaced Repetition:** You must review vocabulary and phrases manually

6. **No Conversation Practice:** You must find conversation partners elsewhere

7. **Self-Assessment Only:** Your level is based on your own perception

8. **No Grammar Lessons:** You must study grammar separately

9. **No Pronunciation Training:** You must use other resources for this

10. **No Vocabulary Building System:** You must create your own vocabulary system
